Wobbles Cobblepot

Purchasing Clerk
Purchasing Department
College of Drystone

Description: A middle-aged gnome who has recently become a smaller version of himself. His jacket is good quality — it was bought when things were better — but it no longer fits quite right across the shoulders. Ink-stained fingers. Eyes that have learned not to linger on anything too long. He keeps a small framed picture of a vineyard on his desk, turned slightly away from him, as if he is not ready to look at it directly but also cannot bring himself to put it away.

Personality: Wobbles was, before the collapse, a cheerful and optimistic administrator with a genuine passion for Zil wine culture. That version of him is still in there but buried under several layers of exhaustion and low-grade dread. He is not a bad person. He made one bad decision after a series of bad decisions made by market forces beyond his control. He is deeply, quietly afraid — not of violence, but of the specific Zil variety of consequences: being noticed, being known, being recorded.

History: Born in Korranberg to a family of minor functionaries, Wobbles worked his way into a comfortable mid-level position in Purchasing over twenty years of reliable, unremarkable service. He developed a serious interest in Zil viticulture on the side — wine tastings, small investments in local vineyards, a subscription to two different wine periodicals. When the AngryCat 30/30 winery launched its fortified reserve vintage as an investment vehicle, Wobbles put in everything he had, including savings he had no business touching. The winery collapsed within fourteen months. AngryCat 30/30 turned out to be mostly the concept of wine rather than wine.

He has told no one at the college. His desk, his paperwork, his attendance record — all perfectly normal. He has been waiting for something bad to happen ever since he signed for that crate.

Above Table: Wobbles was approached through an intermediary — a note slipped under his office door with an amount written on it and instructions to expect a delivery from a supplier named Yardley. He never met the person who hired him. He does not know what was in the crate. He filed it under "Janitorial Supplies, Miscellaneous" because that is what the label said and because the money was already in his account by the time the crate arrived and he had already spent most of it on back rent.

He will confess everything if given any reasonable social pressure. He has been waiting for someone to ask. The relief of telling someone is almost worth the consequences.

He still has the original note. It is in the lining of his jacket. He has not thought about whether to mention this.
